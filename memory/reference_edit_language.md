## What repeats across the references

### Hook

- The premise arrives immediately, usually in the first 1–2 seconds.
- Hooks use a tension pattern: “you’re missing…”, “here’s how…”, “the method…”, or a contrarian claim.
- The first caption is treated as a title card, not a transcript dump: few words, large scale, clear contrast.

### Captions

- One keyword owns the frame; supporting words stay quieter.
- White is the base; one accent color is reserved for the idea being sold.
- Type changes are intentional: bold sans for claims, serif/italic for editorial authority, compact labels for diagrams.
- Captions often sit above the lower third so the face, product, or example remains visible.

### Visual rhythm

- Talking-head footage is interrupted by proof: UI screens, diagrams, maps, product shots, charts, and environmental B-roll.
- Cuts are motivated by a new idea, not just by time. The visual changes when the sentence changes.
- Punch-ins, light leaks, blur transitions, and graphic cards create chapter boundaries.

### Marketing structure

1. Hook / problem
2. Promise or contrarian insight
3. Demonstration, proof, or examples
4. Simple takeaway
5. Branded CTA: save, comment, follow, or learn more
